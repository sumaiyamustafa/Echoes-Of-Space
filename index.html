THE STORY SO FAR 1960s — TODAY

Not every explorer
returns to Earth.

Since the 1960s, NASA missions have left instruments, landers, rovers, and spacecraft across the solar system. Some stopped working. Some completed their goals. Others continue to travel, quietly carrying our questions farther from home.

This is a story about what those machines did, what they taught us, and why their work still matters.

THE MOON

Footprints & instruments

Hardware left by Apollo missions helped scientists study lunar soil, moonquakes, and the Moon's interior long after astronauts went home.

MARS

Robots that read rocks

From Spirit and Opportunity to Curiosity and Perseverance, robotic geologists have searched for evidence of ancient water and habitable environments.

DEEP SPACE

Messages from the edge

Voyager's instruments have studied regions far beyond the planets, helping us understand the space between the stars.

BEYOND THE HARDWARE

Why it matters.

A machine can outlive its mission. The knowledge it sends home can outlive the machine.

01

We learn how worlds work

Instruments measure dust, radiation, rocks, temperatures, and quakes—clues that reveal how planets and moons formed.

02

We search for life's ingredients

Rovers and landers look for past water and environments that could have supported microbial life.

03

We make the next mission smarter

Every success and failure teaches engineers how to build safer, tougher, more capable explorers.
